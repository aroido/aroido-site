Release notes are often treated like a receipt. We prefer them to act more like a decision log.

If an update only says that a button moved or a panel was refreshed, the reader still does not know why the change mattered.

Useful release notes usually answer three things:

- What changed
- What problem it removes
- What the team should watch next

That framing turns updates into working context. It helps new readers catch up faster, and it forces us to be more precise about the actual benefit of the work.
